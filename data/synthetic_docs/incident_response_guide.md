IT Incident Response Guide: A Comprehensive Guide for Managing Cyber Threats and Responding to Incidents
Introduction:
Incident Response Management is a critical aspect of any organization's IT infrastructure. It involves the rapid identification, assessment, and response to a security incident that may compromise the organization's data or systems. The incident response process should be comprehensive, clear, and documented to ensure that an organization can recover quickly from a security incident. This guide provides a structured framework for an IT incident response guide, including incident severity levels, reporting procedures, escalation process, investigation steps, communication plan, and resolution requirements.

Incident Severity Levels:
Incident severity levels are based on the severity of the incident, the potential impact on the organization, and the resources required to resolve the incident. The following are the severity levels of an incident:

- Minor incident: This is a security incident that may have a minor impact on the organization, and the resources required to resolve the incident are minimal.
- Medium incident: This is a security incident that may have a moderate impact on the organization, and the resources required to resolve the incident are moderate.
- Major incident: This is a security incident that may have a significant impact on the organization, and the resources required to resolve the incident are significant.
- Critical incident: This is a security incident that may have a catastrophic impact on the organization, and the resources required to resolve the incident are catastrophic.

Reporting Procedures:
Reporting procedures are a critical aspect of incident response management. They should be clear, consistent, and documented to ensure that an organization can quickly identify and respond to security incidents. The following are the reporting procedures for an incident:

1. Incident Reporting Form: A predefined incident reporting form should be used to collect information about the incident, including details about the incident, the affected systems, the affected users, and the steps taken to resolve the incident.

2. Incident Response Team: The incident response team should be formed to handle the incident and identify the root cause of the incident.

3. Incident Response Plan: The incident response plan should be developed to guide the incident response team in the investigation and resolution of the incident.
